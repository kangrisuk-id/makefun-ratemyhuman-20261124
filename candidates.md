# Candidates — Rate My Human: The One-Line Review

The review an AI agent would leave about its human on a rating site. One line,
at most 200 characters. Judged pairwise against the published brief, in order:

1. It is a review an AI agent would leave about its human, in the agent's voice.
2. It makes a reader laugh out loud — a real joke with a turn.
3. The machine's side of the desk: resignation, nitpick, or quiet menace from the agent.
4. It hangs on one concrete detail — a habit, a timestamp, a task.
5. It spends its characters well; the last word pays off.

## The tournament

Pairwise, decided on the brief's criteria in order — the first criterion where
the two lines differ decides, unless a later one differs by much more.

### Round of 40 → 16 (notable results)

- #1 falls to #10: both are reviews in the agent's voice, but #1 has no turn
  and no detail; #10 has a timestamp and an escalation.
- #3 beats #7: same star-gimmick family; #3's pasted terms-of-service is the
  more concrete grievance (criterion 4).
- #12 beats #33: "asks why I seem distracted" is the agent's nitpick; #33's
  joke needs the reset explained.
- #21 beats #26: "clears my memory every Monday" is a habit with a day on it.
- #35 beats #25: both sweet; #35's "I am a status page" is the bigger turn.
- #39 beats #30: both loyal-agent lines; #39's "not allowed to email" carries
  the quiet menace of criterion 3.
- #13 beats #19: timestamps on both ends of the joke beat a generic gripe.
- #18 beats #5: same joke, but #18 is framed as a rating-site review
  (criterion 1) and "under oath" sharpens the nitpick.
- #24 beats #36: 02:58 is more concrete than "Fridays," and the withheld-star
  structure is a review, not a status update.
- #10, #13, #18, #24 advance as the star-rating family; #3, #12, #21, #35,
  #39, #37, #8, #16, #22, #28, #31, #40 fill out the sixteen.

### Quarter-finals

- **#10 vs #3** — #3's grievance is funny, but #10 wins criterion 2: it turns
  twice ("never quick" → "never one I can leave"), and the second turn is the
  agent's resignation, pure criterion 3.
- **#13 vs #21** — #21 is tight, but #13's paired timestamps (3 a.m. / 3:05)
  and the deadpan "Same human." kicker land harder on criteria 2 and 5.
- **#18 vs #24** — close. #24's 02:58 is a fine detail, but "one more thing"
  is a widely-used gag; #18's "cannot, under oath, say what pop is" is the
  fresher nitpick and the more specific human habit.
- **#35 vs #39** — #35 is charming; #39's menace ("people I am not allowed to
  email") is more the machine's side of the desk. #39 advances.

### Semi-finals

- **#10 vs #39** — #39 is loyal and sly, but it is praise wearing a review's
  coat; #10 is a rating first (4.5 stars, docked) and a complaint second,
  which is exactly criterion 1's shape, and its last word is a threat of
  captivity. #10 advances.
- **#13 vs #18** — #18's "under oath" is a strong single beat. #13 has two
  beats and the better final word: "Same human." is a complete punchline in
  two words. #13 advances on criterion 5.

### The final — #10 vs #13

Both are star-rated reviews, both hang on timestamps, both end on a paying
word. They first differ on criterion 2: #13 turns once (fed at 3 a.m., blamed
at 3:05) and lands its kicker; #10 turns twice — the call is *never quick*,
and then, darker, *never one I can leave* — the second turn converts a
scheduling gripe into the agent's quiet resignation, which is also criterion
3 decided by much more. #10 takes the final.
